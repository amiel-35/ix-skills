# Success criteria — physical works

Read this when the deliverable is built or installed: construction and renovation works, an
installation (heating, network, solar panels), a fit-out, a piece of equipment made to order. It
adds to the method of the skill; it does not replace it.

Physical works invert the usual coverage: little is `mécanique` in the sense of a script, much is
measured on site with an instrument, and the worst defects show only after a season or a year. The
criteria must therefore say **when** each check can be made, not only what it checks.

## 1. Before the works: the criteria that make acceptance possible

- **Every requirement has its acceptance test written before work starts**: what is measured, with
  which instrument, by whom, against which threshold (a performance, a tolerance, a level). A
  requirement without its test at order time is argued about at handover (`relecture`; who: the
  client's technical adviser, not the contractor).
- **The reference documents are named and current**: the plans, the technical rules and standards
  that apply, the manufacturer's instructions; a line checks that the version used on site is the
  version in the contract (`relecture`).
- **The starting state is recorded**: photographs and measurements of the existing site, so a
  later claim about damage or about what was already there can be settled (`terrain`, dated).

## 2. During the works: what disappears

Some things can only be checked before they are covered: insulation before the plasterboard,
reinforcement before the concrete, pipes before the screed, waterproofing before the tiles. Each is
a **hold point**: a `terrain` line at a stage of the schedule, with a photograph or a measurement
kept, and work does not go on until it is passed. A criteria list that checks only the finished
surface passes what is hidden under it.

## 3. At handover

- **Each acceptance test is run and its result recorded** (pressure test, airtightness, electrical
  checks, flatness, output, noise), by someone other than the team that did the work, or witnessed
  by the client (`terrain`).
- **Reservations**: every defect found at handover is listed, dated and assigned, and the handover
  says which ones block use. « Accepted without reservations » while defects are visible is a
  failure of the check, not a success of the works.
- **The paperwork exists**: as-built plans, test reports, manuals, warranties and certificates
  required by the contract or the law (`mécanique` as a checklist; `relecture` for their content).
- **Safety and compliance** are checked by whoever is entitled to (an inspection body where one is
  required) — never certified by the contractor alone.
- **The site is clean** and the surroundings in the recorded starting state (`terrain`).

## 4. After handover: the defects that come later

Write dated `terrain` lines for what only time shows: a heating season for a heating system, a
winter for waterproofing and damp, a year for cracks and settling, the first bills for a
performance promise. Align them with the warranty periods of the applicable law (in France, the
one-year completion warranty, then the longer liabilities), so a defect found is found while it can
still be claimed. The outcome — the building does what it was for, at the promised running cost —
is a zone measured at those dates.

## 5. Unrepresented parties and hidden part

- **Who is not at the table**: the neighbours (noise, access, damage), the future occupants, the
  people who will maintain the installation, the next owner. One line: what the works cost them, and
  who checked it.
- **Hidden part**: the judge chooses on the day which hold points and which rooms are inspected,
  repeats a measurement with an instrument of the judge's own, or has an independent expert sample a few
  points; announcing in advance exactly which spots will be checked invites finishing those spots
  only.

## 6. Usual coverage

Low for a script, high for measurement: most lines are `terrain` with an instrument, at a stage of
the works or at a later date. The paperwork and the arithmetic of the quote are `mécanique`.
