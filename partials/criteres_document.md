# Success criteria — written deliverables

Read this when the deliverable is a text that someone will read and judge: a sales proposal or a
tender response, a dissertation or an essay, a report or a note, a section of a specification
(cahier des charges) that others will answer. It adds to the method of the skill; it does not
replace it.

A text is where « done » is easiest to fake: every section can exist and say nothing. Most lines are
`relecture`; the `mécanique` ones are worth writing anyway, because they are cheap and they catch the
embarrassing failures.

## 1. Lines for any text

- **It answers the question asked**, in its exact wording: the subject of the dissertation, the
  requirements of the consultation, the question of the commission. Check: a reader who has only
  the question and the text says which part of the question each section answers; a part of the
  question that no section answers is a failure, and so is a text that answers a neighbouring
  question (`relecture`; who: someone who did not write it).
- **The main claim can be stated in one sentence** by that reader, and it is the one the author
  meant (`relecture`).
- **Every quotation, figure and reference exists and says what the text makes it say**: draw a few
  at random and open the source (`relecture`; `fact-check` and `calc-check` can do it).
- **No leftover from another text**: no other client's name, no other subject's example, no
  « [à compléter] », no comment left in the margin, no tracked change — searched in the file as sent,
  not in the draft (`mécanique`).
- **Its own rules are kept**: length, format, mandatory parts, deadline, file names, signatures
  (`mécanique` when the rules are written; `controle-qualite` can run them).
- **The objection is met**: the strongest objection a hostile reader would raise is in the text,
  with an answer (`relecture`; the judge can have `red-team` or `contrarian` find it, as hidden
  part).

## 2. Sales proposal, tender response

- **Deliverable**: the offer. Build a compliance matrix from the consultation: one row per
  requirement, the place in the offer that answers it, and « compliant / partly / not »; every row
  filled (`mécanique` once the matrix exists), every « partly » explained (`relecture`).
- **The same figures everywhere**: prices, quantities, dates and staffing identical between the
  technical part, the price schedule and the commitment letter (`mécanique`).
- **The price holds**: costs recomputed, margin above the floor set by whoever commissions the offer
  (`mécanique`, `calc-check`).
- **Outcome**: being shortlisted, then winning at a margin above the floor. A zone, set before
  writing: winning at a loss is not a success.
- **Hidden part**: a reader plays the buyer with the buyer's own grid (the published award
  criteria and their weighting) and scores the offer without the authors present; the score and the
  three weakest answers go to the judge.

## 3. Dissertation, essay, academic work

- **Deliverable**: the text. The author is the student, the judge the teacher or the jury; the
  grading grid is often announced, and then it is the visible plan.
- **The subject, not a neighbouring one**: the terms of the subject are defined, its tension stated
  as a question, and the plan answers that question (`relecture`).
- **Each part moves the argument**: a reader can say what each part adds to the previous one; a
  part that only lists is flagged (`relecture`).
- **Sources and quotations** exist, are cited the way the discipline requires, and are not misread
  (`relecture`, draw a few at random).
- **The work is the author's own**: what the rules allow (tools, help, AI) is stated and kept; an
  oral question on a passage the judge chooses is the usual check (`terrain`, hidden part).
- **Outcome**: the grade, and what the student can do afterwards with the method; a zone set by
  the teacher, not by the student.

## 4. A section of a specification, terms of reference, call for tenders

Here the criteria themselves are a deliverable: they will be published and other people — the
bidders — will write against them. Two consequences.

- **No hidden part on the bids.** Public procurement rules generally require the award criteria and
  their weighting to be announced to every candidate, and fairness asks the same in a private
  tender: check the applicable rules. The hidden part moves to the specification itself.
- **Test the criteria before publishing them**:
  - each requirement is verifiable by the buyer from what a bidder will hand in (no « innovative »,
    « high quality » without an observable) (`relecture`);
  - each award criterion separates good bids from bad ones: write two fictitious bids, one that
    serves the need and one that only ticks the boxes, and score both; if the box-ticking bid wins or
    ties, the grid is gameable (`relecture`, hidden part of the buyer);
  - the requirements do not name a solution or a supplier unless that is a justified constraint
    (`relecture`);
  - the weighting reflects what the buyer actually cares about: the person who owns the need ranks
    the criteria blind, and the ranking matches the weights (`relecture`);
  - every requirement says how it will be checked at delivery, so the supplier's acceptance tests
    follow from the specification (`relecture`).
- **Outcome**: enough compliant bids, and a delivered result that meets the need; measured at
  award, then at acceptance of the delivery.

## 5. Usual coverage

Low to medium: the leftovers, the rules of form, the matching figures and the compliance matrix are
`mécanique`; whether the text answers the question, holds its argument and convinces its reader is
`relecture`; the outcome (grade, award, decision) is decided by others.
